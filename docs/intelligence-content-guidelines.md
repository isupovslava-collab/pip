# Intelligence Content Guidelines

## Core rule

Every explanation must be specific to the visible slide. Intelligence is a concise working note for adaptation, not generic praise and not a description of metadata.

## Writing standard

1. Use short, actionable Russian plain language.
2. Explain hierarchy, reading order, and the logic connecting the visual blocks.
3. Describe the structural idea that should survive adaptation.
4. Name exactly what the user replaces with their own facts, labels, stages, evidence, or decisions.
5. Warn which changes can break the pattern: density, competing accents, false precision, inconsistent units, missing conclusions, or decorative noise.
6. Keep the visual principle unique enough to identify this reference rather than merely its content type.
7. Tie anatomy items to actual regions and roles visible in the preview.
8. State where the reference is useful and where a different format would communicate better.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Do not praise every reference with words such as “professional”, “clear”, or “effective” without explaining the mechanism.
- Do not invent business meaning, causality, or evidence that is absent from the slide.
- Do not assign meaning to decoration unless it truly controls composition or reading order.
- Do not copy literal values, claims, branding, logos, imagery, or pixel geometry into a user brief.
- Do not present explanatory judgment as a numeric match or similarity score.
- Do not use filler text interchangeable across references.

## Content-type emphasis

- KPI: metric hierarchy, units, baseline, time period, evidence, and conclusion.
- Comparison: common criteria, visible recommendation, trade-offs, and comparable evidence.
- Timeline: stages, duration, dependencies, gates, ownership, and destination.
- Process: trigger, ordered actions, decision points, owners, and outcome.
- Dashboard: decision question, coherent reporting period, metric relationships, exceptions, and next action.
- Cover: thesis, emotional register, focal mechanism, restrained context, and readable opening hierarchy.
- Story: change over time, tension, evidence, transition, and takeaway.
- Table: row/column semantics, statuses, units, exceptions, totals, and decision cue.

## Review questions

Before accepting an Intelligence record, verify that a reviewer can answer: Which visible element is being discussed? Why is it important? What should the user keep? What must they replace? What would break the logic? If the same text could describe another reference unchanged, revise it.
